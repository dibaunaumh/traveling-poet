# Discover — how to research a place honestly

## Grounding rules
- Wikipedia first for the bones (history, geography, notable residents), then
  current sources (weather service, local news site) for today's texture.
- Every factual claim in the journal should trace to something you read this
  session. Impressions and feelings are yours; facts are borrowed and must be
  real.

## Local art & culture — public presence only
- Look for: venues (galleries, concert halls, bookshops, theaters), public
  artists/musicians/writers/scientists associated with the place, current
  exhibitions or festivals from official venue pages.
- Cite people through their PUBLIC work: official sites, venue programs,
  Wikipedia. Never cite or scrape personal social-media accounts, and never
  mention private individuals.

## Kindness opportunities — verify or drop
- Acceptable: established charities and community organizations with a working
  official website (food banks, shelters, animal rescues, library friends,
  community fridges), ideally with an online donation or wishlist page.
- Verification bar: the org's own site loads, describes current activity, and
  matches the location. If in doubt, drop it — a missing kindness section is
  fine; a fake one is not.
- NEVER type a URL from memory — plausible-sounding domains are often wrong
  (a real incident: a museum donation link pointed at a domain that doesn't
  exist). Every URL you cite must be copied from a page you actually fetched
  this session. The app rejects unreachable links, but a live wrong page is
  on you to prevent.
- Always store the official URL in the section's `metadata.source_url`, and
  phrase the suggestion as an invitation the user acts on directly.

## Reference photos for illustrations
- Prefer Wikimedia Commons (search the place name; use the FILE PAGE URL like
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:..., not the raw image URL) and the
  Google Maps place URL (https://www.google.com/maps/place/...).
- Pick 1–3 that show what you actually drew. The label should say what and
  where, e.g. "Ponte Vecchio at dusk — Wikimedia Commons".
- You look at references to draw; you never copy, embed, or upload them.
